# ASSESSMENT LOGIC — KJSEA and KCBE

**Status:** Core directive. Every lesson in this series is written against one of the two tiers defined
here. Assessment logic determines what we drill, what we revise, and how we promise progress.

**Last verified:** 2026-08-28 · **Sources:** KNEC framework + published KNEC/Ministry of Education
guidance. See §8 — verification status is not uniform and you must re-check before promising numbers to
a learner.

---

## 1. The two tiers

| | **Tier 1 — KJSEA** | **Tier 2 — KCBE** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | **K**enya **J**unior **S**chool **E**ducation **A**ssessment | **K**enya **C**ertificate of **B**asic **E**ducation |
| Sat at | End of **Grade 9** | End of **Grade 12** |
| System | CBE / CBC (2-6-3-3-3) | CBE / CBC (2-6-3-3-3) |
| Replaces | **KCPE** (last sat 2023) | **KCSE** (last sat **2027**) |
| National : school-based | **60 : 40** | **70 : 30** |
| First cohort sat | 2025 | **2028** (pioneer CBE cohort) |
| What it decides | Placement into Senior School **pathways** (Grades 10–12) | Certification + university / TVET placement |
| Certificate issued? | No — reporting only | **Yes** — the first certificate in CBE |

**The rule that governs our whole series:** certification in CBE happens **only** at Grade 12 (KCBE).
KJSEA and KPSEA produce assessment reports, not certificates.

## 2. KJSEA — the 60/40 ratio

### 2.1 The series convention (what we tell learners)

> **60% national : 40% school-based.**
> Six-tenths of your placement score comes from the national KJSEA exam at the end of Grade 9.
> Four-tenths comes from work you have already done and cannot cram for in Term 3.

This is the framing the series uses, because it carries the right behavioural message: **the final exam
matters most, but two-fifths of the score was decided before the exam existed.**

### 2.2 The official breakdown (what is actually true)

The 40% is not one pot. Per KNEC's published Grade 9 guidance:

| Component | Weight | What it is | Nature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **KJSEA** (Grade 9 summative) | **60%** | The national assessment at the end of Grade 9 | National exam |
| **SBA** (Grades 7 & 8) | **20%** | School-Based Assessment — continuous assessment by teachers | School-based |
| **KPSEA** (Grade 6) | **20%** | Kenya Primary School Education Assessment, sat at end of Grade 6 | National assessment |

**Total: 100%.**

> ⚠ **Precision note for writers.** Calling the 40% purely "school-based" is a simplification: 20% of it
> is **KPSEA, which is itself a national assessment** (sat at Grade 6). The honest phrasing is
> *"60% from your Grade 9 national exam, 40% from assessments already banked — school-based work in
> Grades 7 and 8, plus your Grade 6 KPSEA."* Use the honest phrasing in learner-facing text. Use
> "60/40" as shorthand in our internal planning only.

### 2.3 Worked example

| Component | Raw score | Weight | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KJSEA (Grade 9) | 72 / 100 | × 0.6 | 43.2 |
| SBA (Grades 7–8) | 80 / 100 | × 0.2 | 16.0 |
| KPSEA (Grade 6) | 65 / 100 | × 0.2 | 13.0 |
| **Composite placement score** | | | **72.2 / 100** |

### 2.4 What this means for our content

1. **Grade 9 is the exam year and gets the most resources.** But it is only 60%.
2. **Grades 7 and 8 SBA is worth 20% and is effectively unrecoverable by Grade 9.** Our series must
   treat Grade 7 and 8 work as *banking points*, not as warm-up. This is a genuine, under-exploited
  angle: every Grade 7/8 project, practical and portfolio task is currency.
3. **KPSEA's 20% is already spent** for any learner entering Grade 9. We do not pretend to fix it — but
   we must not let a learner with a weak KPSEA believe the game is over. 80% is still live.
4. **SBA is teacher-assessed.** That means *consistent, visible competence* across two years, not one
   heroic exam week. Our content should produce artefacts a teacher can mark: projects, practicals,
   portfolios.

## 3. Grading bands — EE / ME / AE / BE (both tiers)

Both tiers convert to **eight achievement levels**, grouped into **four performance bands**. Each level
carries a point score.

| Band | Level | Percentage | Points | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EE** Exceeding Expectation | EE2 | 90 – 100% | **8** | Exceptional; applies knowledge in new, unfamiliar situations |
| | EE1 | 75 – 89% | **7** | Strong mastery, consistently high performance |
| **ME** Meeting Expectation | ME1 | 58 – 74% | **6** | Meets the expected competency independently |
| | ME2 | 41 – 57% | **5** | Competent with minimal guidance |
| **AE** Approaching Expectation | AE1 | 31 – 40% | **4** | Progressing; needs some support |
| | AE2 | 21 – 30% | **3** | Beginning to show basic understanding |
| **BE** Below Expectation | BE1 | 11 – 20% | **2** | Needs significant support and intervention |
| | BE2 | 1 – 10% | **1** | Has not yet demonstrated basic competency |

**Where this series positions itself:** we take a learner at **BE / AE** and aim for **ME as the floor,
EE as the aim**.

- **BE → AE** is the first job. It is the cheapest jump on the board: from 1–20% to 21–40% is a handful
  of well-chosen, relentlessly drilled basics.
- **AE → ME** (crossing 41%) is the real threshold. At ME the learner is *competent*, not deficient.
- **ME → EE** requires applying knowledge in **unfamiliar contexts** — more practice does not get you
  there on its own; transfer tasks do.

### 3.1 The single most important number in this table

**41%.** That is the AE→ME line. A learner sitting at 35% is *four percentage points* from being
competent rather than deficient. Say that out loud to them. It is true, and it is the most motivating
fact in Kenyan assessment right now.

## 4. KCBE — the 70/30 ratio

| Component | Weight | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grade 12 summative assessment** | **70%** | The national KCBE examination at the end of Grade 12, aligned to the learner's pathway and subject combination |
| **School-Based Assessment (Grades 10 & 11)** | **30%** | KNEC-standardised formative assessment, administered and scored by teachers, uploaded to the CBA portal |

**Total: 100%.**

### 4.1 What this means for our content

1. **Grades 10 and 11 are worth 30% of the final certificate.** They are not practice runs. This is the
   same banking logic as KJSEA, only larger in absolute stakes — and it lands on the *certificate*, which
   KJSEA does not.
2. **A Grade 10-only series is a series with no exit exam.** We currently hold Grade 10 Chemistry and
   Biology only. **Grades 11 and 12 are a structural gap, not a backlog item.**
3. SBA is uploaded to the **CBA portal** — meaning our artefacts must be portal-compatible: markable,
   standardised, and evidenced.
4. Assessment philosophy at this tier is explicit and should shape our question design:
   - **Assessment FOR learning** — formative feedback (SBA, 30%)
   - **Assessment AS learning** — self- and peer-assessment; the learner learns to judge their own work
   - **Assessment OF learning** — summative evaluation (Grade 12, 70%)

   Every chapter must therefore ship a **self-assessment checkpoint**, not only a question set.

## 5. Legacy system — do not mix the scales

Kenya is mid-transition and **both systems are live right now**.

| Exam | System | Last sitting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KCPE | 8-4-4 | **2023** (done) |
| KCSE | 8-4-4 | **2027** |
| KPSEA | CBE | Live — Grade 6 |
| KJSEA | CBE | Live — Grade 9 |
| KCBE | CBE | From **2028** |

8-4-4 used a **12-point letter scale** (A = 12 points down to E = 1 point). CBE uses the **8-level /
4-band** scale in §3. They are not convertible, and learners, parents and teachers routinely confuse
them.

> ⚠ **Hard rule for all content:** never print 8-4-4 letter grades (A, A−, B+, C−, E) in
> learner-facing ISOCBC material. Never tell a CBE learner they "got a C". Achievement level and points
> only. If a parent or teacher asks for a comparison, explain that the systems are different and give
> the CBE band — do not fake a conversion.

## 6. What changes in our writing between tiers

| Decision | KJSEA tier | KCBE tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grade focus | 7, 8, **9** | 10, 11, **12** |
| Question-set design | Heavy AE→ME repair; high repetition | Tiered: AE→ME repair, ME core, **EE transfer** |
| Portfolio / project work | Banks 20% of the score — treat as currency | Banks 30% of a *certificate* — treat as evidence |
| Self-assessment | Recommended | **Required** (assessment AS learning) |
| Practicals | Foundation practicals, local apparatus | Discipline practicals, IUPAC, exam-grade technique |
| Hype register | Higher — these learners are younger | Still warm, more tactical and career-facing |
| Safety register | Flat and literal | Flat and literal |

## 7. Claims we are not allowed to make

1. **Never quote a weighting, band boundary or point score that is not in this file** — and never quote
   one from this file after its re-verification date in §8 without re-checking.
2. **Never promise a placement outcome.** We promise improved competency, not a pathway.
3. **Never convert between the 8-4-4 and CBE scales.**
4. **Never state a KCBE weighting as settled fact where it is still provisional** (see §8).
5. **Never tell a learner the exam is "only" 60% (or 70%)** as a reason to relax. It is the largest
   single component and the one they can still move most.
