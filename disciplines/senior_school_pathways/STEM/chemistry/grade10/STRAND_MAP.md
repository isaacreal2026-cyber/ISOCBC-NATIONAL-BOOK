## STRAND 1.0 — INORGANIC CHEMISTRY

### 1.1 Introduction to Chemistry — 6 lessons

**Content:** definition of chemistry · branches of chemistry · careers in chemistry · chemistry in daily
life (agriculture, pharmaceutical industry, medicine, manufacturing, food industry, entertainment,
nuclear chemistry).

**Specific Learning Outcomes** — by the end, the learner should be able to:
- a) explain the meaning of Chemistry as a field of science
- b) explore the role of Chemistry in day to day life
- c) **examine the effects of drug and substance use** in day to day life
- d) promote the rights and responsibilities to a safe and healthy learning environment

**Key Inquiry Questions:** *(verify verbatim against the design PDF before drafting)*

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Outcome **c) is mandatory** — drug and substance use is a required PCI, not an add-on.
- Careers link is explicit in the design (digital literacy: searching for Chemistry careers).
- Design-flagged competencies to hit: digital literacy, citizenship (consumer rights, prescription
  information), critical thinking.
- ⚠ Outcome (d) is a values/rights outcome. Treat it seriously, keep the sibling tone, drop the jokes.

### 1.2 The Atom — 24 lessons

**Specific Learning Outcomes** — by the end, the learner should be able to: describe the structure of the
atom; explain the meaning of isotopes and relative atomic mass; illustrate electron arrangement; predict
the type of ion formed from a given electron arrangement; *(confirm the full verbatim list from the
design before drafting)*.

**Key Inquiry Question:** *How are electrons arranged in an atom?*

**Design-flagged details**
- Electron arrangement using **s and p notation** for the first 20 elements.
- Core competencies: learning to learn (independent practice of s/p notation), digital literacy
  (animations/simulations/videos), critical thinking (predicting ion type).
- Values seen in the design: social justice, integrity, peace.
- PCIs: environmental conservation (model the atom from **locally available materials**), life skills
  (self-esteem while presenting), global citizenship (contributions of scientists to atomic theory).

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Prime candidate for the "map, not the territory" guardrail — the Bohr/shell model gets patched later.
- Isotopes and relative atomic mass are a classic BE/AE cliff. Extra repair reps go here.

### 1.4 Chemical Bonding — 24 lessons

**Content:** bond types and intermolecular forces — ionic, covalent, dative covalent, hydrogen bond,
Van der Waals, metallic · Lewis / dot (·) and cross (×) diagrams · physical properties of giant ionic,
simple molecular, giant atomic/covalent and giant metallic structures (solubility, thermal and electrical
conductivity, melting point, boiling point).

**Specific Learning Outcomes** — by the end, the learner should be able to:
- a) illustrate bond types in elements, molecules and compounds
- b) investigate the relationship between bond types and physical properties of elements, molecules and
  compounds
- c) relate bond types and resultant structures to the uses of elements, molecules and compounds
- d) appreciate the uses of different substances based on their bond types and structures in day to day
  life

**Key Inquiry Question:** *How do chemical bonds influence the properties of substances?*

**Core competencies:** learning to learn (independent dot-and-cross practice) · digital literacy
(animations/simulations) · creativity and imagination (model building) · critical thinking and problem
solving (inferring bond type ↔ structure).

**Values:** love (care for others during physical-property investigations).

**PCIs:** environmental conservation (**locally available materials**) · life skills / creative thinking
(modelling SiO₂, NaCl, graphite, diamond) · social cohesion (group discussion of bond types).

**Required project:** model bonding in selected molecules/compounds — e.g. NaCl, SiO₂, graphite,
diamond — **using locally available materials**.

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- This is the worked example already in `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4 (giant ionic lattice). Reuse that spine.
- Big trap zone: "molecules" for ionic compounds; confusing intermolecular forces with intramolecular
  bonds; hydrogen bond called a "bond inside the molecule".
- ⚠ Properties investigations involve heating and conductivity apparatus — safety language goes flat.

### 1.5 Periodicity — 32 lessons

**Content:** gradation in size of atoms and ions, appearance, ionisation energy, electron affinity,
melting and boiling points · ductility, malleability and electrical conductivity.

**Specific Learning Outcomes** — by the end, the learner should be able to:
- a) describe the trends in physical properties of elements of the periodic table
- b) investigate the chemical properties of elements in groups of the periodic table
- c) describe the trends in properties across a period
- d) outline applications of elements of the periodic table
- e) appreciate applications of various elements of the periodic table

**Key Inquiry Questions:**
1. *Why is the study of chemical families important?*
2. *How does the position of an element in the periodic table influence its properties?*

**Core competencies:** communication and collaboration (group discussion of trends) · self-efficacy
(confident plenary presentation) · learning to learn (independent practice writing balanced equations).

**Values:** unity (appreciating each group member during experiments).

**PCIs:** learner support programmes (healthy inter- and intra-personal relationships) ·
socio-economic & environmental (**safety in class and school environment — preparing chlorine gas**) ·
life skills / assertiveness (presenting findings).

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Largest single sub-strand (32 lessons). Pace it or it swallows the term.
- ⚠ **Chlorine gas preparation is in this sub-strand.** This is the single highest-safety-risk item in
  Grade 10 Chemistry. Highest-safety register, teacher-present language, fume cupboard / ventilation,
  and a hard "do not attempt unsupervised" line. No jokes anywhere in this lesson.
- Group I / II reactions with oxygen, chlorine, cold water, steam and dilute acids — gas collection and
  testing are exam staples; give them dedicated reps.

## Design Assessment Rubric (KICD's own indicator rows)

The published Grade 10 Chemistry rubric includes, among others:

| Indicator | EE | ME | AE | BE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ability to explain the importance of chemistry in day to day life | correctly explains ≥ 4 | ≥ 3 | ≥ 2 | ≥ 1 |
| Ability to illustrate the electron arrangement of elements using s and p notation | all 20 elements | 15–20 elements | 10–15 elements | at most 10 elements |
| Ability to write balanced equations for chemical reactions | all | most | some | few, with prompts |
| Ability to investigate the relationship between bond types and physical properties of elements | correctly and comprehensively | correctly | some bond types, some detail | most detail missing |
| Ability to describe the trends in properties in groups and periods | all trends | most trends | some trends | few trends |

Our chapters must supply equivalent rows for **every** learning outcome we teach — same countable,
observable, incremental structure; our own wording.
