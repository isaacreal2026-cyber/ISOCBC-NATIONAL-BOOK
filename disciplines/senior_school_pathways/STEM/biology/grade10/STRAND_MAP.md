## STRAND 1.0 — CELL BIOLOGY AND BIODIVERSITY

### 1.1 Introduction to Biology — 6 lessons

**Content:** application of Biology · fields of study · careers related to Biology.

**Key Inquiry Question:** ⚠ **verify verbatim from the design PDF before drafting.**

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- First chapter of the book. This is where a BE reader decides whether the book is for them.
- Careers are explicitly in the design: medicine, agriculture, marine science, anthropology,
  environmental studies.
- Design-flagged competencies seen in Grade 10 Biology: critical thinking and problem solving,
  communication and collaboration, learning to learn, digital literacy.

---

### 1.2 Scientific investigations in Biology — 14 lessons

**Key Inquiry Question:** ⚠ **verify verbatim from the design PDF.**

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- This sub-strand carries the scientific method, apparatus, safety, observation, measurement, data
  recording and reporting.
- Highest-leverage chapter in the grade: it is the prerequisite for every practical that follows.
  Do not rush it.
- ⚠ Safety register: apparatus, specimens, fieldwork, hygiene. Flat and literal.
- Design-flagged competencies: critical thinking and problem solving, digital literacy, learning to
  learn (reflection on own work).

---

### 1.3 Cell structure and Specialization — 20 lessons

**Key Inquiry Question:** ⚠ **verify verbatim from the design PDF.**

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Microscope work, plant vs animal cells, organelles, cell specialisation and tissues.
- Anchor gold: onion epidermis is available in every school; cheek cells; a light microscope.
- Classic traps: confusing cell wall / cell membrane / cytoplasm roles; "vacuole" used for both plant
  and animal; organelle vs organ.
- Diagram-heavy chapter — coordinate with `assets/diagrams/` naming convention.

---

### 1.4 Chemicals of life — 24 lessons

**Key Inquiry Question:** *How are chemicals important in…* — ⚠ **verify verbatim; the design's row is
truncated in the accessible copy.**

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, vitamins, enzymes, water — and the food tests.
- Food-test practicals (starch/iodine, reducing sugars/Benedict's, proteins/Biuret, lipids/emulsion)
  are exam staples. Give each test its own colour-result table.
- ⚠ Food tests involve heating and reagents: flat safety register.
- Nutrition content here links directly to the health PCI, and to Strand 3.1 (animal nutrition).

## STRAND 2.0 — ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY OF PLANTS

### 2.1 Nutrition — 12 lessons

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Photosynthesis, mineral nutrition, and — confirm against the design — parasitic/saprophytic nutrition.
- Anchor: a maize shamba, a tea farm, a bougainvillea in a compound.
- Competencies seen in the design: communication and collaboration, critical thinking and problem
  solving, learning to learn.

### 2.2 Transport in plants — 22 lessons

**Content:** structure of the plant transport system in external parts — roots, stems and leaves.

**Key Inquiry Question:** *How are materials transported in plants?*

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Xylem/phloem, transpiration, translocation, root hair absorption, wilting.
- Locally-available-material anchor: celery or a white flower in coloured water, a balsam plant.
- Classic trap: "plants have a heart / plants have blood". Kill it explicitly.

### 2.3 Gaseous exchange and Respiration — 22 lessons

**Key Inquiry Question:** *Why is gaseous exchange important in plants?*

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Stomata, lenticels, guard cells, root hairs, respiration vs photosynthesis.
- Classic trap: confusing gaseous exchange with transport, and confusing respiration with breathing.
  This confusion is the single most persistent BE-level error in the whole grade.

---

## STRAND 3.0 — ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY OF ANIMALS

### 3.1 Nutrition — 12 lessons
**Notes for MKUBWA** — links to `1.4 Chemicals of life`. Digestive system, enzymes, balanced diet,
deficiency diseases. Health PCI is explicit and natural here.

### 3.2 Transport — 24 lessons
**Notes for MKUBWA** — circulatory system, heart, blood components, blood vessels, lymph.
⚠ Any dissection work: flat safety register, and treat specimen handling with respect.

### 3.3 Gaseous exchange and Respiration — 24 lessons

**Content:** characteristics of respiratory surfaces of animals.

**Key Inquiry Questions:**
1. *How does gaseous exchange occur in animals?*
2. *Why is…* — ⚠ **verify the second question verbatim from the design PDF.**

**Notes for MKUBWA**
- Largest animal sub-strand (24 lessons). Pace it.
- Respiratory-surface characteristics: thin, moist, well-ventilated, large surface area, well-supplied
  with blood. Those five features are the mark-scheme core — make them a memorisable list.
- Classic trap: learners describe *the lung* instead of *the characteristics*. Teach the examiner's list.

## Cross-grade notes

- **Mirror structure with Plants (Strand 2):** Nutrition → Transport → Gaseous exchange. The parallel is
  a feature, not a coincidence — use it. Teach plants first, then say *"animals run the same three
  problems, different hardware."*
- **Diagram demand is highest at 1.3, 3.2 and 3.3.** Budget diagram production early — see
  `assets/diagrams/README.md`.
- Design pedagogical requirement across the grade: Inquiry-Based, Project-Based and Problem-Based
  Learning, with hands-on activities as the constructivist core.
