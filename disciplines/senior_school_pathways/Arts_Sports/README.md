## Why this cluster is different — and why our format must change

Everything in this cluster is **assessed by performance and portfolio, not by written paper.** A
learner does not "know" a piece of music; they *play* it. They do not "know" a dance; they *perform* it.

**Consequence for this series:** the strand map → lesson → question-set spine we use for Chemistry and
Biology does not transfer here unchanged. This cluster needs:

- **Performance rubrics** instead of question sets — anchored on the same EE/ME/AE/BE bands
- **Portfolio artefacts** as the primary evidence, not as an add-on
- **Practice progressions** (warm-up → drill → repertoire → performance) in place of concept blocks
- **Video/diagram-heavy instruction** — see `assets/diagrams/`; dance notation, choreography, staging
  diagrams and musical notation are diagram dependencies we do not currently have

## Content warning — plan before writing

This cluster will consume **diagram and media assets faster than any other** (`assets/diagrams/` is
currently empty). Budget asset production before drafting, or the lessons will be unwritable.

There is also a **rights question**: music, choreography, scripts and film extracts may carry copyright
or cultural-heritage restrictions. Resolve the rights position before embedding third-party material.
Prefer original compositions, traditional material used with proper attribution, and material we
commission.

## Notes for MKUBWA

- **The sibling voice works especially well here**, because this is the pathway learners choose out of
  love. Match their energy — but keep the dignity rule absolute.
- **Never describe a learner's body as the problem.** In Sports and Physical Education especially, we
  coach capacity and technique, never physique. This is a hard boundary.
- ⚠ **Safety register is high here.** Injury, overtraining, warm-up, hydration, equipment use, and safe
  training load: flat, literal, imperative. No jokes. Coaching enthusiasm never outranks a safety line.
- Talent is not the entry ticket. Practice progression is. Say so, repeatedly — it is the single most
  encouraging true thing we can tell this reader.
