## Scope note — one paper, several traditions

Social Studies at junior school integrates what 8-4-4 treated separately: **History & Citizenship,
Geography, and elements of Civics / Religious Education**. Treat it as one integrated subject, not three
mini-subjects stapled together — but know which tradition each skill comes from, because the exam
techniques differ:

- **History-flavoured items** reward chronology, cause and consequence, and evidence.
- **Geography-flavoured items** reward map and data skills, and physical–human interaction.
- **Civics-flavoured items** reward values, rights, and citizenship reasoning.

Religious Education is examined **separately** (CRE `908`, IRE `909`, HRE `910`). It is *not* part of
`907` and is not scaffolded in this folder.

## Notes for MKUBWA

- **Highest-yield repair area: data and map skills.** Interpreting a graph, a table, a photograph or a
  map is a *skill*, it appears every year, and it is routinely under-taught. A BE learner can gain two
  achievement levels on data skills alone.
- **Content-heavy, so it rots.** Names, dates and places are the weakest thing to drill. Teach
  *structure* (cause → event → consequence; physical feature → human activity → consequence) and the
  content has somewhere to stick.
- **Values are genuine here, not decoration.** This is the natural home for patriotism, social justice,
  integrity, unity and national values. But per `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §1, we never moralise *at* the reader —
  we reason with them.
- **Handle politics, ethnicity, religion and land history with care.** Be accurate, balanced and
  age-appropriate. Where an interpretation is contested, say it is contested rather than picking a side.
  This series must be safe to hand to any Kenyan child.
- Local anchors: county examples, local rivers and settlements, devolution, real community institutions.
